Case #0005 — Judith File

The Gentileschi File

1612–1620: Baroque painter Artemisia Gentileschi returns to the same subject more than once — Judith, a widow who saves her besieged city by beheading the enemy general Holofernes, with help from her maidservant. Compare that to Caravaggio's more restrained version, and to Kehinde Wiley's 2012 reinterpretation with a Black woman in Judith's role.

Content — a biblical widow beheads an enemy general to save her city.
Form — dramatic Value contrast (tenebrism) and a diagonal Movement through the composition.
Context — Baroque Italy; Gentileschi trained under her father Orazio and survived a widely documented assault and trial as a young woman, which many historians connect (as one interpretation, not the only one) to the force in her Judith paintings.
Function — biblical heroines like Judith were often commissioned to symbolize courage and virtue.

One-Page Cram Sheet

Form • Content • Context • Function

  • Form — How does it look? Materials, technique, elements & principles used.
  • Content — What is it? Subject matter, story, symbols.
  • Context — Who/where/when made it, and what beliefs shaped it.
  • Function — Why was it made, what was it used for, artist's intention.

Elements of Art (the ingredients)

  • Line, Shape, Color, Value, Texture, Space, Form

Principles of Design (the recipe)

  • Balance, Contrast, Emphasis, Pattern, Unity, Movement, Rhythm

Credit Line Format

  • Artist Name (Birth–Death). "Title". Date Created. Material. Dimensions. Current Location.

Gentileschi Quick Facts

  • Artemisia Gentileschi (1593–1653), trained by her father Orazio; painted Judith Slaying Holofernes more than once.
  • Shares Caravaggio's tenebrism (strong Value contrast); her Judith is more physically engaged than his.
  • Kehinde Wiley reimagined the subject in 2012 with a Black woman in Judith's role.
